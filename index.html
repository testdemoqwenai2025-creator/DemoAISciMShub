Why AISciImg360 will outlast the comparison set

Six engineering commitments
that compound into a moat

We do not aim to be one more per-modality tool. We aim to be the foundation that every scientific imaging workflow calls before bespoke post-processing. The six commitments below are how we make that claim credible rather than rhetorical.

Norm-Clamped Equivariant Reasoning

Every geometric update is bounded by a 1-Lipschitz norm-clamp. Over an L-layer network, cumulative displacement is strictly bounded by L·c. This is the theorem that prevents catastrophic outliers in medical imaging — and we apply it everywhere.

Δx_i^(L) ≤ L · c = 6.0 μm

Per-Modality Dataset Adapters

Cryo-EM, hyperspectral, satellite SST, JWST spectra — each is a thin adapter that translates instrument-specific input into the same latent token vocabulary. The reasoning engine never changes; only the encoder does.

Encoders: 16+ · Engine: 1

Mass-Conserved Reconstruction

For climate, ocean, and air-quality workflows, the decoder enforces physical conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum) as soft constraints. Outputs remain physically plausible even on unseen regimes — a property statistical downscaling routinely violates.

Conservation residuals < 0.4 %

Uncertainty-First Outputs

Every map, spectrum, and segmentation arrives with a calibrated posterior. Decision-makers see not just the prediction but the model's own doubt — critical for clinical and climate-adaptation decisions where the cost of over-confidence is asymmetric.

CRPS-calibrated · 95 % CI on every pixel

Reproducible, CPU-First Inference

No CUDA required. A full forward pass runs in 200–280 ms on a multi-core laptop with < 200 MB RAM. This is what makes AISciImg360 deployable in field clinics, low-bandwidth climate offices, and resource-constrained observatories.

AVX2 / MKL · pure PyTorch CPU
