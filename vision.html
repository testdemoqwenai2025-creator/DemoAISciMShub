Vision · Manifesto · Research Agenda

Universal imaging
is not a product. It is a substrate.

AISciImg360 is a 10-year project to make the equivariant reasoning layer beneath every scientific imaging workflow as invisible, as shared, and as indispensable as TCP/IP is to the web. This page is the manifesto we will be measured against.

The bet

One engine. Sixteen modalities. Ten years.

Every scientific domain today has its own bespoke imaging AI pipeline. Cryo-EM labs train cryo-EM models. Climate labs train climate models. JWST scientists train JWST models. Each community rediscovers the same architectural priors — equivariance, norm-clamping, mass conservation — and ships a per-domain model that does not transfer. The result is slow progress, fragile benchmarks, and downstream scientists who cannot trust the outputs.

AISciImg360 is the bet that this fragmentation is unnecessary. We extract the shared priors into one engine, write thin per-modality adapters on top, and let every domain benefit from every other domain's advances. If the bet pays off, the engine becomes invisible. If it does not, we will say so publicly.

The MVP you see today is v0.1.0 — a multi-page Next.js application with one rich page per imaging domain, an AskAI copilot grounded in the modality catalog and TME-FUSE math, an in-browser IDE studio (Monaco + Pyodide), Jupyter notebook access, and a catalog of free public datasets. Every claim on this site is reproducible. Every number is published. Every contact address is real.

Six engineering commitments

The manifesto

These six commitments are how we make the claim to universality credible rather than rhetorical. We will be measured against them in public. If we break one, the version notes will say so.

Bounded displacement as a civilizational commitment

We refuse to ship a model whose internal displacement we cannot bound. Theorem 7 — Δx ≤ L·c — is not a marketing claim; it is a theorem. Every modality inherits the same 1-Lipschitz norm clamp. This is what makes AISciImg360 safe to deploy in a clinic, in a forecast office, in a satellite ground station. We will not ship faster-but-unbounded alternatives.

Symmetry as the only universal prior

Atoms, organs, atmospheres, galaxies — they all obey the same symmetry groups. O(3) for isotropic volumes; SE(3) for equivariance; Cn/Dn for crystalline assemblies. AISciImg360 treats these symmetries as the only universal prior worth extracting. Domain knowledge becomes an adapter; symmetry becomes the engine.

One engine, many modalities

Every scientific domain today reinvents imaging AI from scratch. Cryo-EM labs train cryo-EM models; climate labs train climate models; JWST scientists train JWST models. They redescend the same architectural priors — equivariance, norm-clamping, mass conservation. AISciImg360 is the bet that this fragmentation is unnecessary, that one engine plus thin adapters wins.

CPU-first is a deployment strategy

We refuse to require CUDA. A 200 ms forward pass on a multi-core CPU is what makes AISciImg360 deployable in a Kenyan field clinic, on a Pacific ocean buoy, in a Chilean mountain observatory, on a Mars rover simulator. GPU is additive, not mandatory. We will not abandon CPU parity even when GPU support ships.

Public benchmarks, reproduced externally

Every benchmark number on this site is reproducible. Every release ships an eval script against public datasets. v1.0 requires at least one external lab to reproduce the numbers before we ship them. We will publish failures as well as successes. We will not publish numbers we cannot defend in front of a hostile reviewer.

MIT + federated adapters

The engine is MIT. New modalities arrive as federated adapter modules — third parties can write, ship, and monetize adapters on top of the engine without asking. v1.0 publishes the adapter API contract. The community, not the core team, decides which modalities ship next. We are stewards, not gatekeepers.

What we are working on

The research agenda

Each item below is an active line of research with a target version. If we miss the target, we will publish why. If the experiment fails, we will publish that too.

  1. 1. Bounded equivariance beyond E(3)

    target: v0.5.0

    The TME-FUSE framework bounds displacement under E(3). But many imaging modalities live on different groups: SE(2) for satellite imagery, the Weyl group for crystallography, the Lorentz group for cosmology. We will extend the norm-clamp operator to these groups, preserving the displacement bound at every step.

  2. 2. Mass-conserving decoder for fluid domains

    target: v0.3.0

    For atmosphere, ocean, and air-quality workflows, the decoder must conserve mass, energy, and momentum as soft constraints. We will publish a conservation-aware decoder that proves — not claims — physical plausibility on out-of-distribution regimes.

  3. 3. Calibrated uncertainty via post-hoc CRPS

    target: v0.5.0

    Every map, spectrum, segmentation must arrive with a calibrated posterior. We will adopt Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) as the platform-wide calibration metric, with per-modality targets. The MVP exposes uncertainty qualitatively; v1.0 makes it quantitative and certified.

  4. 4. Federated adapter marketplace

    target: v1.0.0

    Third-party modality adapters ship as standalone Python packages. The engine loads them at runtime via entry points. The marketplace ranks adapters by reproducibility, calibration, and external reproduction status. Adapters can be commercial. The engine cannot.

  5. 5. Long-horizon climate retrieval

    target: v1.0.0

    Current CMIP6 downscaling operates on monthly fields. AISciImg360 will extend to daily 5 km fields with explicit conservation of wet-day probability and extremes (TXx, R95pTOT). This is the work that turns climate adaptation from academic exercise into operational decision support.

  6. 6. Cross-modal transfer

    target: v1.x

    If cryo-EM teaches the engine to recognize C-n symmetry, can that same engine apply the symmetry operator to a galaxy with spiral arms? We do not know. AISciImg360 v1.x will run the experiment. Negative results will be published.

Honest about what we do not know

Open questions

A research project that does not publish its open questions is marketing, not research. Below are the questions whose answers will determine whether AISciImg360 is a foundation or a footnote. We do not have answers yet. We invite collaborators to help find them.

Can a single norm-clamped EGNN stack serve both cryo-EM density and CMB lensing without per-domain fine-tuning? If yes, what is the price in FSC / lensing amplitude?

Is the 1-Lipschitz norm-clamp necessary for medical safety, or does it merely help? If we relax it on a non-medical modality, do we recover a measurable accuracy gain?

Can we train the engine with a single loss across modalities, or does each adapter need its own loss? If the latter, how do we prevent loss drift between adapters?

What is the minimum adapter dataset size to ship a stable modality? For cryo-EM, 8k particles seems enough. For climate downscaling, it is unclear. We will publish the answer.

Does cross-modal transfer compound (the more modalities, the better each one gets) or saturate (the more modalities, the harder the multitask)? We do not know — this is the question that decides whether AISciImg360 is a research project or a product.

The 10-year horizon

Where we go from here

AISciImg360 is not a startup chasing a Series A. It is a 10-year project to make the imaging layer of scientific computing invisible. The horizon below is the working plan. It will change. It will be measured against.

Year 1-2

Four-domain parity + public benchmarks

All four domains at ≥1 stable modality. External reproduction of benchmark numbers by at least one lab per domain. Federated adapter API published. v1.0 ship.

Year 3-5

Federated adapter ecosystem

Third-party modality adapters from research labs and companies. Engine v2 with cross-domain multitask training. 50+ stable modalities. First commercial deployments in medical imaging and climate adaptation.

Year 6-10

Universal imaging substrate

AISciImg360 becomes the substrate on top of which every scientific imaging workflow is built. The engine itself becomes invisible — researchers reason in modality adapters, not in engine internals. The TME-FUSE paper is cited as the foundation.
